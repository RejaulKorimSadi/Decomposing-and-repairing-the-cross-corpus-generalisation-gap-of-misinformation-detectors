\begin{letter}{The Editors-in-Chief\\Information Processing \& Management}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the manuscript ``Mostly ranking, partly threshold: decomposing and repairing the cross-corpus generalisation gap of misinformation detectors'' for consideration as a research article in \emph{Information Processing \& Management}.

Fake-news and rumour detectors report near-perfect accuracy on the corpus they were trained on and much lower accuracy on others. Recent benchmarks and audits have established \emph{that} the gap exists. Our paper asks \emph{what kind} of gap it is and what can be done about each part of it, which we believe fits the journal's interest in the evaluation and reliability of information-processing systems. In particular:

\begin{itemize}
\item We audit ten public corpora in English and Bengali (articles, headlines, claims, tweets) for label direction and for near-duplicates within and across corpora. Among other things, the audit shows that the two most used article corpora code their labels in opposite directions, an easy mistake that turns moderate transfer into an apparent below-chance collapse.
\item We split the transfer gap exactly into a ranking deficit and operating-point losses. Across 42 corpus pairs and seven detectors, including a fine-tuned transformer, \RankShareAll{} of the lost macro-F1 is ranking (corpus-bootstrap interval \RankShareCI) and \OpShareAll{} is operating point. The split holds with balanced accuracy and with a cross-fitted oracle.
\item We show that the standard label-shift correction (EM prior adaptation) makes the operating point worse because calibration does not survive the change of corpus, and that flagging items at the source base rate recovers \RecRank{} of the loss without target labels. A short proposition states when this rule is exact, and a controlled experiment shows how it degrades when the base rate shifts.
\item In a leave-one-corpus-out design, training on several corpora helps ranking, while making the representation invariant to corpus identity hurts, because corpus and label are entangled in current benchmarks.
\item We extend the analysis to Bengali, a low-resource language, where clickbait detection holds up on unseen publishers and partly across languages.
\end{itemize}

Results come with repeated seeds, bootstrap intervals, permutation controls and paired tests. Every number in the manuscript is generated from released result files, and the code, seeds and per-item scores are public. The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. Both authors contributed equally, approved the submission and declare no competing interests. A generative-AI declaration is included in the manuscript as required by the journal.
