\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Cross-domain failure of fake-news detectors}
Content-based detectors have been known to degrade across domains since the first multi-domain corpora appeared. Models trained on one news domain lose much of their accuracy on another \citep{perezrosas2018automatic,janicka2019crossdomain}, and fine-tuning on one COVID-19 corpus often hurts performance on related ones \citep{suprem2022generalizability}. \citet{bozarth2020evaluation} showed that which model looks best depends on the dataset, the evaluation type and the metric. \citet{delloglio2026comparison} recently benchmarked twelve approaches on ten English corpora and found cross-dataset median F1 close to 0.5 for classical and fine-tuned models, with zero-shot large language models more stable; \citet{hoy2025features} found stylistic and ``social-monetisation'' features to transfer better than token representations. These studies show \emph{that} detectors fail to transfer, almost always through a single threshold-dependent score such as accuracy or F1. They do not ask whether the loss is one of discrimination or of a mis-set threshold, and that question is the basis of this paper.

\paragraph{Multi-domain and debiased detectors}
A separate line of work builds detectors that cope with several domains or with drift by design. EANN trains an event discriminator adversarially so that features carry less event-specific information \citep{wang2018eann}; MDFEND mixes domain experts with a domain gate \citep{nan2021mdfend}; and ENDEF removes the contribution of an entity-only model at test time to generalise to future news \citep{zhu2022endef}. All of them assume that the domain-specific part of the signal is a nuisance that can be separated from the veracity part. Our repair experiments test that assumption directly on public benchmarks and find that, in these corpora, the two are largely the same thing (Section~\ref{sec:repair-res}).

\paragraph{Shortcuts and dataset artefacts}
Shortcut learning, in which a model exploits a cue that predicts the label only in the training distribution, affects deep and shallow models alike \citep{geirhos2020shortcut,gururangan2018artifacts,mccoy2019right}. For misinformation, \citet{horne2017thisjustin} and \citet{schuster2020stylometry} showed how much of the signal is style rather than veracity, and \citet{asr2019bigdata} documented topical imbalance across fake-news corpora. \citet{verma2026audit} audited ISOT in depth: subject metadata alone separate the classes perfectly, a newswire tag marks 99.2\% of real articles, duplicates contaminate 19.4\% of a naive test split, deleting the 1{,}000 highest-weight unigrams leaves F1 at 0.926, and ISOT-trained models rank LIAR claims at close to chance. \citet{thibault2025guide} surveyed 75 misinformation datasets and found keyword--label correlations and temporal leakage in several, including Twitter15/16, ISOT and BanFakeNews. We build on these audits but with a different scope and aim. We audit ten corpora jointly, including duplication across corpora and label direction, and use the audit to explain and repair the transfer gap rather than only to document it.

\paragraph{Rumour benchmarks}
Rumour-detection benchmarks have the same problem. On Twitter15/16 and PHEME, event-level spurious correlations inflate scores \citep{wu2022spurious}, chronological splits lower them \citep{mu2023time}, and simple text baselines rival propagation-based models \citep{pelrine2021simple}. We therefore treat the source posts of these corpora as one more text unit and do not re-evaluate propagation models, which these works already cover.

\paragraph{Label shift, quantification and invariant representations}
Two families of methods from domain adaptation map onto the two parts of our decomposition. Label-shift adaptation re-estimates the target class prior from unlabelled predictions and adjusts the posteriors \citep{saerens2002adjusting,lipton2018bbse}. With calibrated scores, this EM procedure is hard to beat \citep{alexandari2020labelshift}. The quantification literature studies the closely related task of estimating class prevalence in a new sample; adjusted classify-and-count, which corrects the share of predicted positives with the classifier's source error rates, is its standard baseline \citep{forman2008quantifying}. Both families assume that the class-conditional distribution of inputs, or at least of scores, stays the same between source and target. Representation methods try instead to remove domain information: correlation alignment matches feature covariances \citep{sun2016coral}, adversarial training removes domain-predictive directions \citep{ganin2016dann}, and iterative nullspace projection deletes linearly decodable attributes \citep{ravfogel2020inlp}. Domain-adaptation theory bounds target error by source error plus a divergence that a domain classifier can estimate \citep{bendavid2010theory}. We know of no misinformation study that measures how much of the cross-corpus gap is a prior or threshold effect and how much a representation effect, or that tests each family on the part it is meant to fix.

\paragraph{Large language models}
Zero-shot and few-shot large language models are less exposed to dataset-specific shortcuts because they are not trained on the benchmark \citep{pelrine2023gpt4,thibault2025guide,delloglio2026comparison}, and they can advise smaller detectors \citep{hu2024badactor}. They also change the target: machine-written misinformation can be harder for both people and detectors to spot than human-written misinformation \citep{chen2024llmmisinfo}, which makes generalisation beyond the training distribution more pressing. Supervised detectors nonetheless remain the cheapest and most widely deployed option, and our decomposition applies to any detector that outputs a score, a prompted language model included.
